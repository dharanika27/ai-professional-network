# Resume Writing Guide

## The Power of Action Verbs

Every bullet point in your work experience section should begin with a strong action verb in past tense. Weak verbs like "helped," "assisted," "worked on," or "was responsible for" drain the energy from your accomplishments. Replace them with verbs that convey ownership and impact.

High-impact action verbs by category:

**Leadership and management:** Led, Directed, Managed, Oversaw, Mentored, Coached, Recruited, Hired, Developed, Spearheaded, Championed

**Building and creating:** Built, Designed, Architected, Developed, Created, Established, Launched, Implemented, Deployed, Engineered

**Improving and optimizing:** Reduced, Increased, Improved, Streamlined, Optimized, Accelerated, Transformed, Revamped, Modernized, Automated

**Delivering results:** Achieved, Delivered, Exceeded, Surpassed, Generated, Produced, Secured, Won, Negotiated, Closed

**Analyzing and researching:** Analyzed, Researched, Identified, Evaluated, Assessed, Diagnosed, Investigated, Quantified, Modeled

## Measurable Outcomes and the STAR Framework

Each bullet point should follow an implicit STAR structure: Situation (implied from context), Task (your role), Action (what you specifically did), Result (the measurable outcome). You don't need to label each element — weave them into a single powerful sentence.

Weak: "Worked on improving the checkout process."
Strong: "Redesigned checkout flow by eliminating 3 friction steps, increasing conversion rate from 2.1% to 3.4% and generating $1.2M in additional annual revenue."

Quantify with these types of metrics:
- **Percentages:** "reduced churn by 23%", "increased throughput by 60%"
- **Dollar figures:** "$4.5M cost savings", "managed $800K budget"
- **Volume/scale:** "served 2M daily active users", "processed 50K transactions/day"
- **Time savings:** "cut release cycle from 3 weeks to 4 days"
- **Headcount:** "led team of 12 engineers", "hired and onboarded 8 SDRs"

If you don't have exact numbers, use reasonable approximations: "approximately 40% reduction," "roughly $500K in annual savings." Approximations are better than vague claims.

## Resume Length Guidelines

For professionals with fewer than 10 years of experience: one page, maximum. For senior professionals, directors, and technical architects with 10-20 years of experience: two pages is appropriate. Executives and academics may use longer formats (CV), but standard corporate roles should stay at two pages.

Ruthlessly cut older or irrelevant experience. Roles more than 15 years old can be condensed into a single line: "Earlier career: Database Administrator at Acme Corp (2003-2008)." Your most recent 5-7 years of experience should occupy 75% of your resume's space.

## Tailoring Resumes to Job Descriptions

A generic resume sent to 50 jobs produces fewer callbacks than 5 tailored resumes sent to 5 carefully chosen roles. For each application:

1. Read the job description thoroughly — identify the top 5 required skills
2. Ensure those exact skills appear in your Skills section and experience bullets
3. Adjust your Summary statement to match the role's seniority and focus area
4. Reorder your bullet points so the most relevant achievements appear first within each role
5. Mirror the company's language — if they say "agile methodology," use that phrase, not just "scrum"

A tailored resume takes 20-30 minutes per application and dramatically increases interview rates.

## Skills Section Strategy

The Skills section serves two purposes: ATS keyword matching and quick scanning by recruiters. Structure it for both audiences:

Organize skills into categories:
- **Languages:** Python, TypeScript, Go, SQL
- **Frameworks:** FastAPI, React, Django, Node.js
- **Infrastructure:** AWS, Docker, Kubernetes, Terraform
- **Databases:** PostgreSQL, Redis, MongoDB, Elasticsearch
- **Tools:** Git, Jira, Figma, Tableau

List your proficiency-ordered skills first within each category. Do not include skills you would struggle to demonstrate in an interview — it backfires when you cannot answer questions about something on your own resume.

## Writing a Compelling Summary Statement

The summary statement (also called professional summary or profile) appears at the top of your resume and is read first. It should be 2-4 sentences that compress your most marketable qualities into a tight narrative.

Formula: [Years of experience] + [Job title / domain] + [2-3 strongest technical skills] + [signature achievement or differentiator] + [what you're looking for].

Example: "Senior Software Engineer with 8 years building distributed data pipelines at scale. Expert in Python, Apache Spark, and AWS. Reduced data processing costs by $1.1M at DataCo by redesigning batch ETL architecture to streaming. Seeking Staff Engineer roles focused on data infrastructure at high-growth Series B+ companies."

Avoid generic phrases: "results-driven professional," "passionate about technology," "excellent communication skills." These phrases appear on millions of resumes and add no signal.
